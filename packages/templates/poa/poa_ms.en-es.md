Verified May 31, 2026 against MS Code Title 87 Ch. 3 (Uniform Durable Power of Attorney Act), Title 25 Ch. 34 (Revised Mississippi Law on Notarial Acts), and Title 89 Ch. 3 (Forms of Acknowledgment). Do not modify the items in this block without re-verifying against the current primary sources.

**Governing statute.** Mississippi Uniform Durable Power of Attorney Act, Miss. Code Ann. §§ 87-3-101 to 87-3-115 (Title 87, Chapter 3). This is the older UDPAA — **not** the 2006 Uniform Power of Attorney Act (UPOAA). Mississippi has not adopted UPOAA. Do not import UPOAA § 119 (acceptance immunity), § 120 (refusal penalties), § 114 (codified agent duties), or §§ 201–217 (statutory subjects-of-authority shorthand). UDPAA has no statutory equivalents.

**Durability magic words.** The instrument must contain language showing intent that authority continue notwithstanding subsequent disability or incapacity. Acceptable formulations per § 87-3-105:

- "This Power of Attorney shall not be affected by my subsequent disability or incapacity." (immediate)
- "This Power of Attorney shall become effective upon my disability or incapacity." (springing)

Springing POAs are expressly recognized at § 87-3-115 (added 2018 HB 277).

**Notary acknowledgment — long form (natural person).** Form per Miss. Code Ann. § 89-3-7 and the Revised Mississippi Law on Notarial Acts, §§ 25-34-1 to 25-34-57 (eff. July 1, 2021). Mississippi authorizes traditional wet-ink (TWIN) and in-person electronic (IPEN) notarization. Mississippi does **not** currently authorize remote ink-signed (RIN) or remote online (RON) notarization; the principal must physically appear before the notary. Long-form text used in this template (above) is the verified form.
