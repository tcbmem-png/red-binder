## VERIFIED — locked

Verified May 31, 2026 against TN Code Title 34 Ch. 6 Pt. 1 (Uniform Durable Power of Attorney Act) and TN Code Title 66 Ch. 22 (Acknowledgment of Instruments). Do not modify the items in this block without re-verifying against the current primary sources.

**Governing statute.** Tennessee Uniform Durable Power of Attorney Act, Tenn. Code Ann. §§ 34-6-101 to 34-6-112 (Title 34, Chapter 6, Part 1). This is the older UDPAA — **not** the 2006 Uniform Power of Attorney Act (UPOAA). Tennessee has not adopted UPOAA. Do not import UPOAA § 119 (acceptance immunity), § 120 (refusal penalties), § 114 (codified agent duties), or §§ 201–217 (statutory subjects-of-authority shorthand). UDPAA has no statutory equivalents. § 34-6-102 is the definition of "durable power of attorney"; § 34-6-103 binds the principal to the agent's acts; § 34-6-104 governs revocation; § 34-6-105 keeps the DPOA in force notwithstanding a later conservatorship; §§ 34-6-108/109 address scope over property.

**Durability magic words.** Per § 34-6-102, the writing must contain language showing intent that authority continue notwithstanding subsequent disability or incapacity. Acceptable formulations:

- "This Power of Attorney shall not be affected by my subsequent disability or incapacity." (immediate)
- "This Power of Attorney shall become effective upon my disability or incapacity." (springing)

**Notary acknowledgment — long form (natural person).** Form per Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-22-107, with general saving provision at § 66-22-114 (any certificate clearly evidencing intent is valid; exact wording recommended but not required). Commission expiration must appear on the certificate (§ 8-16-115). Tennessee authorizes audio-video appearance under the saving language attached to §§ 66-22-107/108/114; the form must read "personally appeared before me by audio-video communication" in lieu of "personally appeared before me." **Default is in-person. Audio-video is an option, not required.**

**Execution requirements (Part 1 financial DPOA).** No statutory witness or notarization requirement (contrast Part 2 health-care DPOA at § 34-6-203, which requires notary OR two witnesses with attestation). Notarization is effectively mandatory in practice — recording with the register of deeds requires it, and most banks require it.

**Cite-as footer line.** Tennessee Uniform Durable Power of Attorney Act, Tenn. Code Ann. §§ 34-6-101 to 34-6-112 (Title 34, Ch. 6, Pt. 1). Notarial acknowledgment per Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-22-107, with general saving provision at § 66-22-114.
