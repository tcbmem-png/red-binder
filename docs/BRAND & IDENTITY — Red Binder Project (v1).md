# Brand & Identity — Red Binder Project (v1)

_Working answers to the three open questions in the marketing brief: naming, visual identity (and the TCB Law tie), and entry points. Built from the brief, the op-ed, and the privacy principles. Drafts, not law — meant to be argued with. Drafted 2026-06-17._

---

## The one-line position

**A plan, before the emergency.** / **Un plan, antes de la emergencia.**

Everything else hangs off that. We are not selling safety, hope, or a way to stay. We are handing someone a steady thing to hold in an unsteady time. The product is memory, not transformation — we make the next right action easier to find, on the worst day, when no one can think straight.

A warmer companion line for community and social, the way families actually talk about this:

**Just in case.** / **Por si acaso.**

"Por si acaso" does quiet work the English can't. It's how a mother already frames this to herself. It carries love and realism without a drop of alarm.

## 1. Naming

### The principle

Names in this project do two jobs at once: they have to be calm and dignifying in English **and** land the same way in neutral Latin American Spanish at an 8th-grade reading level. A name that needs a footnote to translate is the wrong name. And no name should say the worst outcome out loud. We don't put "detention" on a thing a scared person carries in their pocket.

### Umbrella — keep it simple

The project is the **Red Binder Project** / **Proyecto La Carpeta Roja**. The tool is just "your Red Binder." Don't invent a separate app name — "Builder," "portal," "platform" all add a layer the user has to learn. The verb is plain: **start your Red Binder / empieza tu Carpeta Roja.** One name, one thing, one shelf.

> Spanish note: "binder" has no clean one-word equivalent. *Carpeta* (folder/binder) is the most common and warmest; *carpeta roja* reads naturally. Confirm with a native bilingual reviewer before lock — some regions say *fólder* or *cartapacio*, but *carpeta* travels best.

### The documents inside

Give each document a plain-language public name and keep the legal name for the document itself:

| What it is (legal) | Public name (EN) | Public name (ES) |
|---|---|---|
| Family emergency overview | **The Red Binder Plan** | **El Plan de la Carpeta Roja** |
| Durable Financial Power of Attorney | **Who handles money & home** (POA) | **Quién maneja el dinero y la casa** (poder) |
| Detention card ("DD") | **Pocket Plan** — see below | **Plan de Bolsillo** |

### The card — replacing "DD"

**Recommendation: Pocket Plan / Plan de Bolsillo.**

Why it's the right name:

- It never says "detention." It names what the thing *is for you* — a plan you carry — not the worst day that might make you reach for it.
- It's on-brand to the bone: a plan (the whole project's promise), made before, small enough to hold.
- It translates clean and warm. *Plan de bolsillo* is natural, dignified, 8th-grade-readable.
- It covers both physical pieces honestly: the **Pocket Card** you carry, and the **Binder Page** you keep at home and with a trusted person. Same plan, two places.

So the structure is:

- **Pocket Plan** (the product) =
  - **Pocket Card** / **Tarjeta de Bolsillo** — what you carry. The one rule and the short script. No sensitive information on it, on purpose.
  - **Binder Page** / **Página de la Carpeta** — kept safe at home and with someone you trust. Your details so a lawyer can find you, your decision, and the authorization that lets your people and a lawyer act fast.

**Strong alternative to weigh: The Red Card / La Tarjeta Roja.** Upside — it builds a color family with the Red Binder, and "red card / tarjeta roja" is already a trusted know-your-rights object in immigrant communities, so it arrives pre-understood. Downside, stated honestly — the ILRC "red cards" are an established, widely distributed thing; calling ours a Red Card risks confusion about who made it and what it does (ours sets up counsel authorization, theirs is rights-only). If you want the color family, I'd still lead with **Pocket Plan** as the product name and let "red" live in the visual system, not the card's name. Your call, and worth a quick check with the immigration-org folks you already know before deciding.

### One naming rule to hold the line

The honest-limits language from the brief is a naming constraint too. Nothing gets a name that implies it's finished or that it protects you by existing. A generated PDF is not "your POA" until it's signed and notarized. A Pocket Card doesn't keep anyone in the country. Names stay modest so the disclaimers don't have to fight them.

## 2. Visual identity

### The idea

The hero asset already exists and it's perfect: a bright red binder on a shelf — unmistakable, easy to grab, easy for a child or a relative to find. The op-ed makes the case better than a brand deck could. So the identity isn't invented; it's the binder, made consistent. Red here is **functional, not alarmist** — it's the color of the thing you can find in a panic. We lean into it, but we warm it so it reads as steady and human, not emergency-siren.

### Color

A confident, slightly warm true red — cardinal, not blood, not stop-sign. Crucially, it must sit clearly apart from your other two systems.

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Binder Red** | `#C8362B` | The one bold color. Mark, spine, key buttons, headers. Use it like punctuation, not paint. |
| **Bone** | `#FBF7F1` | Paper / page ground. Warm near-white. |
| **Ink** | `#1F1B1A` | Body text. Near-black, slightly warm. |
| **Steady Gray** | `#6B6360` | Secondary text, captions, the calm voice. |
| **Quiet Green** | `#3F6B53` | Sparingly — "done / signed / ready" confirmations only. The one moment of relief. |

Kept deliberately distinct from the neighbors:
- **TCB Law oxblood** `#7A1F2B` is darker, browner, formal. Binder Red is brighter and rounder. Side by side they're clearly different families.
- **gotcb orange** `#B8442A` leans orange/rust. Binder Red leans true-red/cool. Different temperature.

A user should never wonder if they're on a law-firm site or a consultancy site. They should feel like they're holding a community tool.

### Type

Recommendation: **Atkinson Hyperlegible** for UI, body, and the cards.

It's not a fashion pick — it's a values pick that happens to look good. It was designed for maximum legibility including for low-vision readers, its letterforms are hard to confuse (critical on a wallet card read in a stressful moment, and across two languages with accent marks), and it's free and open-source — which matches the project's whole posture. Using a typeface built for "everyone can read this" *is* the brand argument.

For the wordmark, set "Red Binder Project" in a sturdy, plain weight of the same family (or a simple grotesque) — no script, no flourish. The dignity is in the plainness.

This also keeps the three systems straight: TCB Law is Fraunces/Inter, gotcb is Georgia/Helvetica, Red Binder is Atkinson Hyperlegible. Three distinct typographic voices, no blending.

### The mark

Keep it iconic and reproducible in a single color, because it has to survive a notary stamp, a one-color flyer, a volunteer's t-shirt, and a 1-inch app icon. The natural mark is a **red binder spine with a label** — or stripped further, a **red ring-binder tab**. One shape, one color, works at any size. Avoid anything that needs gradients or fine detail.

### What red does and doesn't touch

Red is the find-me color, so it earns its place on the binder, the mark, and the single most important action on a screen. It does **not** become the background of pages of dense legal text, where it would read as alarm. Calm pages, bold red anchors. That contrast *is* the feeling we want: steadiness with one clear thing to reach for.

## 3. The TCB Law relationship — endorsed by, not branded as

This is the consequential call, so here's the reasoning, not just the answer.

The audience has a well-founded fear of anything that looks official, institutional, or like a system that collects names. A law-firm-forward face works against the privacy promise — it makes the tool feel like an intake funnel. But the attorney credibility is exactly what makes the legal documents trustworthy and the project legitimate. Both things are true.

So: **TCB Law is the quiet endorser, not the headliner.**

- The front face — landing pages, the binder, the cards, social — is the Red Binder Project's own identity. Community tool, not firm.
- TCB Law appears where trust needs a name: the About page, the footer, the legal disclaimers, the "who built this and why" — *"A TCB Law initiative. Built by Taylor C. Berger, attorney (MS/TN)."* Present, sober, reassuring. Never the hero.
- This also protects TCB Law's own letterhead system (oxblood, Fraunces/Inter, TCB.LAW wordmark) from bleeding into a separate brand. Keep them in separate lanes, as the brief asks.

Net effect: a frightened person meets neighbors. A skeptical person — a partner org, a notary, a funder — finds the lawyer's name one click down, exactly where due diligence looks.

## 4. Entry points — two doors, one tool

People arrive in two very different states, and one front door can't speak to both without watering down both. Two landing pages, converging into the same enter-once flow.

### Door A — Planning ("I want to be ready")

For the family thinking ahead: protect the kids, the home, the business if a parent can't be there. Calmer, lower fear. This is also the broad "every family needs this" door from the op-ed — the safest, widest top of the funnel.

- **EN headline:** Make the plan you hope you never use.
- **EN subhead:** Free documents that let someone you trust care for your kids, your home, and your money if you can't be there. Made on your device. We keep nothing.
- **ES headline:** Haz el plan que esperas nunca usar.
- **ES subhead:** Documentos gratis para que alguien de confianza cuide a tus hijos, tu casa y tu dinero si tú no puedes. Se crean en tu teléfono. No guardamos nada.
- First question is gentle and concrete: who would you want to step in?

### Door B — Right now ("I'm scared this could happen")

For acute fear — ICE is active, someone they know was taken. More urgent, still calm, dignity over fear. This is the Pocket Plan door.

- **EN headline:** Know what to do. Don't sign anything until you talk to a lawyer.
- **EN subhead:** A card you carry and a plan that lets your family reach a lawyer fast — set up before you ever need it. Made on your device. We keep nothing.
- **ES headline:** Sé qué hacer. No firmes nada hasta hablar con un abogado.
- **ES subhead:** Una tarjeta que llevas contigo y un plan para que tu familia localice a un abogado rápido — listo antes de que lo necesites. Se crea en tu teléfono. No guardamos nada.
- First action gets the Pocket Card in hand fast; the deeper Binder Page and authorization follow.

### Where they meet

Both doors drop into the same enter-once tool. Conditional intake means a Door A user never sees immigration fields, and a Door B user starts with the card. The privacy line — **made on your device, we keep nothing** — appears on both, every time, in both languages. It is not a fine-print reassurance. It is the headline relationship. (See §5.)

## 5. Privacy is the brand, not a policy page

From the principles doc: the thing that can't be subpoenaed, breached, or turned against you is the thing that was never collected. For this audience, "we keep nothing" *is* the whole relationship — so it gets said plainly, in the UI, at the moment of fear, in both languages, and it's backed by open-source code anyone can check.

Say it three ways, all true, never overstated:

- **Plain:** Made on your device. We keep nothing.
- **Warm:** Your information never leaves your phone.
- **Verifiable:** The code is open. Don't take our word — anyone can check.

Never imply more privacy than the build delivers. If a server path ever exists for one optional feature (per the principles doc), it's disclosed at that exact spot, never the default, and never the only way to get your document.

## 6. Voice — quick reference

Plain words. Periods where others use commas. Calm, direct, humane, honest about limits. We never use fear as a hook — the urgency here is real and external, and we don't manufacture more of it. No funnel-bro language, no scarcity, no "trusted by," no countdown timers. Free, stated plainly. English and Spanish side by side, same dignity in both.

The op-ed is the voice bible. The register to keep: *"No politics. No pressure. Just practical help, offered with respect."* And: *"Sometimes a simple red binder is the difference between panic and a plan."*

### On "¡Make Plan B Aura!"

Honest read, consistent with the brief's own guardrails: the instinct — meet a scared community in its own register, lighten a dreaded task — is right. But "aura" is current slang with a short shelf life, and against the gravity of a parent planning for detention it risks undercutting the weight as a core line. It also runs into the brief's "dignity, not fear / no manufactured anything" rules. If it lives anywhere, it's a wink in youth-facing social — never the cornerstone. The cornerstone is the weight: the village, the plan, the steady thing to hold.
